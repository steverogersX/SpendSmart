## North Star Metric

**Qualified leads captured per week** — defined as a user who completed a full audit AND submitted their email.

This is the single number that matters most at this stage because it sits exactly at the intersection of product value and business value. An audit completed means the product worked. An email submitted means the user found enough value to want to keep the result. Everything else — traffic, shares, signups — is either upstream or downstream of this number.

This is not DAU. This is not pageviews. People use this tool once, maybe twice a year when their stack changes. A tool people use once a quarter should not be measured like a social app. What matters is: did they get value, and did we capture them as a lead?

If this number is growing week over week, everything is working — the product is useful, the distribution is reaching the right people, and Credex has a pipeline of real leads to follow up on.

---

## 3 Input Metrics That Drive the North Star

**1. Audit completion rate**
Formula: Audits completed ÷ Landing page visitors

This tells you if the form is the problem. If people land on the page but don't finish the audit, the input form is too long, too confusing, or too intimidating. Target: above 50%. If it drops below 35%, the form needs fixing immediately.

**2. Email capture rate**
Formula: Emails submitted ÷ Audits completed

This tells you if the results page is delivering real value. If someone completes the audit but doesn't submit their email, they either didn't find the results useful or didn't trust the product enough to hand over their email. Target: above 25%. If it drops below 15%, the results page is not showing enough value — the savings numbers are too small, the recommendations are too vague, or the design is not landing.

**3. High-savings audit rate**
Formula: Audits showing >$500/month savings ÷ Total audits completed

This tells you if the right people are finding the tool. If most audits show tiny savings, either the pricing data is wrong or the wrong users are filling it out — indie devs with $50/month spend rather than startup teams with $2,000/month spend. Target: above 30%. This number also directly predicts how many Credex consultation bookings are possible.

---

## What to Instrument First

In this exact order:

1. Landing page visits — know how many people are arriving
2. Audit start events — know how many people begin the form
3. Audit complete events — know how many people finish it
4. Email submit events — know how many people capture their result
5. Consultation booking clicks — know how many high-savings users engage with Credex CTA
6. Shareable URL clicks — know if the viral loop is alive

Do not instrument everything at once. These six events tell the full story of the funnel. Everything else is noise at this stage.

---

## What Number Triggers a Pivot Decision

**If email capture rate stays below 15% for 3 consecutive weeks after the product is live — that is the pivot signal.**

It means one of two things: either the audit results are not showing meaningful savings (product problem) or the wrong users are finding the tool (distribution problem). Both are solvable but require different responses.

If audit completion rate is high but email capture is low — the product is working but the results page is not convincing. Fix the results page.

If audit completion rate is also low — the form itself is the problem. Simplify it.

If high-savings audit rate is below 15% consistently — the target user is wrong. The tool is reaching people with too little AI spend to make Credex economics work. Repoint distribution toward startup teams instead of indie devs.

**One number. Three weeks. That is the decision window.**
