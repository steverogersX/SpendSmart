## Assumptions before anything else

These are the foundation of every number below. If these are wrong, the math changes.

**About the customer:**
- There are two types of users who will use this tool — indie devs and startup teams
- Indie dev average AI spend: $150–$300/month (API keys + 1–2 coding assistants)
- Small startup team (5–15 people) average AI spend: $1,000–$3,000/month
- We assume a blended average customer spends **$800/month** on AI tools
- Credex offers credits at 40% discount → customer pays **$480/month** through Credex
- Credex sourced those credits at roughly **$400/month** (from overstock companies)
- Credex margin per customer = $480 - $400 = **$80/month**
- Average customer stays **10 months** before churning or no longer needing credits

**About the funnel:**
- Not every audit visitor becomes a lead
- Not every lead books a consultation
- Not every consultation converts to a purchase
- These drop-off rates are realistic, not optimistic

---

## What is a converted lead worth to Credex?

**LTV (Lifetime Value) calculation:**

$80/month margin × 10 months = **$800 LTV per customer**

For a larger startup spending $3,000/month:
- Pays Credex $1,800/month (40% discount)
- Credex sourced at $1,500/month
- Margin = $300/month × 10 months = **$3,000 LTV**

For an indie dev spending $150/month:
- Pays Credex $90/month
- Credex sourced at $75/month
- Margin = $15/month × 10 months = **$150 LTV**

**Blended realistic LTV: $800 per customer**

The tool is most profitable when it converts startup teams, not indie devs. Indie devs are great for top-of-funnel volume and word of mouth, but the economics are driven by the startup customers with larger spend.

## Conversion funnel — what rates make this profitable?

**Stated assumptions for the funnel:**
- 10,000 people see the tool across all channels in month 1
- 12% click through to the landing page (cold audience, realistic CTR)
- 55% of landing page visitors complete the audit (form is simple, value is clear)
- 25% of audit completers enter their email (email asked after value is shown)
- 35% of high-savings leads (>$500/mo savings) book a consultation
- We assume 30% of all audits show >$500/mo savings
- 30% of consultations convert to a credit purchase

**The math:**

```
10,000 people see the tool
↓ 12% click through
1,200 land on the page
↓ 55% complete the audit
660 audits completed
↓ 25% enter email
165 leads captured
↓ 30% of audits show >$500/mo savings = 198 high-savings audits
↓ 35% of those book consultation
69 consultation calls booked
↓ 30% convert to purchase
~21 paying customers
```

**Overall audit-to-customer rate: ~3%**

**Does this work financially?**
- 21 customers × $800 LTV = **$16,800 total revenue from first cohort**
- Cash cost to acquire: $0
- Time cost: ~40 hours across all channels
- **Profitable from day one**

Even at 1% audit-to-customer rate:
660 audits × 1% = 6–7 customers × $800 LTV = $5,600 — still profitable at $0 CAC.

## What has to be true for $1M ARR in 18 months?

**Work backwards from the target:**

$1M ARR
= $83,333/month revenue needed
÷ $80/month margin per customer
= **1,042 active paying customers needed simultaneously**

To reach 1,042 active customers in 18 months assuming 10% monthly churn:
New customers needed per month by month 18 = ~**104 new customers/month**

At 3% audit-to-customer conversion:
104 customers ÷ 3% = **~3,467 audits needed per month by month 18**

Is 3,467 audits/month realistic by month 18?

Honestly — it's a stretch from organic alone. It becomes realistic only if:

1. The shareable result URL compounds — every shared audit brings 3–5 new visitors
2. Credex email to existing customers converts 50–100 customers in month 1, giving the revenue base a head start
3. Blog articles on Medium and the personal domain start ranking for searches like "cursor vs copilot cost" by month 6
4. At least one Hacker News post goes front page — that alone can drive 500–1,000 audits in 48 hours
5. Churn is closer to 5% not 10% — customers keep buying as long as they keep saving

**Month-by-month realistic targets:**

| Month | Audits/month | New customers | Churned | Total active | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 300 | 9 | 0 | 9 | $720 |
| 2 | 400 | 12 | 1 | 20 | $1,600 |
| 3 | 500 | 15 | 2 | 33 | $2,640 |
| 6 | 900 | 27 | 8 | 95 | $7,600 |
| 9 | 1,500 | 45 | 15 | 210 | $16,800 |
| 12 | 2,200 | 66 | 30 | 366 | $29,280 |
| 15 | 3,000 | 90 | 50 | 556 | $44,480 |
| 18 | 3,500 | 105 | 75 | 736 | $58,880 |

**Honest conclusion: $1M ARR in 18 months is unlikely from organic alone.**

$58,880/month by month 18 = roughly **$700K ARR** — close but short of $1M.

To hit $1M ARR in 18 months the tool would need either:
- A paid distribution budget added from month 6 onward
- A viral moment (front page Hacker News, big X account sharing it) that spikes audits to 5,000+ in a single month
- Credex upselling existing customers to higher credit volumes, increasing margin per customer above $80/month

The tool is still a strong business case at $700K ARR with $0 CAC. The $1M target is possible but requires at least one non-organic spike to get there.
